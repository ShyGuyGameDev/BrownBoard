# Three work systems leave the canvas

Linear, Dart, and Atlassian are strongest when work has to become a durable record, and weakest when a small group wants to open a link and think on one surface. As of late October 2026, Linear’s advantage is a fast, opinionated path from a software issue to a pull request: a free tier, then **$10 or $16 per user per month** on yearly Basic and Business, sold as trusted by **more than 40,000 companies**. That same restraint is the weakness. Reporting and permissions stay thin, the product is built for teams shipping software, and the core product pages describe no drawing canvas. Dart, the AI project tool from Keeple, is strongest when a sentence, a forwarded email, or an explicit Slack action becomes a groomed task that a person or an agent can own, starting free for **four teammates** and then **$8 or $12** a teammate on the yearly prices. It is weakest where that AI output still needs an editor, the tracker is shallower than Linear or Jira, and the company lists a team of ten. Atlassian’s advantage is institutional scale — **more than 350,000 customers**, **over 85% of the Fortune 500**, and a Marketplace of **more than 6,000 apps** — plus a simple board in Trello and a real whiteboard inside Confluence. Its weakness is that those two views are different products, gated by editions, admins, and a seat price that stepped up in October 2026, with AI credits billed from December. BrownBoard, in this comparison, is the room none of them sell: one shared link, guests included, a kanban and a freeform whiteboard holding the same tasks, without an issue tracker, an agent that creates work, a wiki, or a marketplace.

## Linear’s speed is one workflow tied to a pull request

Linear’s homepage now calls the product “the product development system for teams and agents,” and the object under that slogan is still the issue. An issue belongs to one team, carries a human identifier such as `ENG-123`, and can be assigned, prioritized, labeled, commented on, placed in a cycle, and attached to one project. Projects roll into initiatives, which on Enterprise can nest **up to five levels** as of July 2025. Cycles are a team’s repeating planning period. Views filter those same issues. Above the issue, the planning stack is milestones, scheduled updates, and an intake inbox: triage can suggest an assignee, team, project, and labels, and can flag duplicates, while Customer Requests attach feedback to issues and projects, including attributes synced from Salesforce. ([Homepage](https://linear.app/)) ([Concepts](https://linear.app/docs/conceptual-model)) ([Sub-initiatives](https://linear.app/changelog/2025-07-10-sub-initiatives)) ([Intake](https://linear.app/intake)) ([Customer Requests](https://linear.app/customer-requests))

The strength reviewers and comparisons keep naming is that this model is hard to misuse. G2’s May 2026 summary rates Linear **4.6 out of 5 from 120 reviews** and credits an intuitive, fast interface plus Slack and GitHub. A September 2025 Product Management thread — 100 points, 77 comments, a six-person team six months after leaving Jira — says the UI is clean and the team uses it without complaining. The highest-voted comment argues people like Linear because it is harder to configure badly than Jira. Breeze describes one good workflow set beside Jira’s workflow engine: keyboard-first, a local-first sync engine, setup in an afternoon, admin burden close to none. Siit’s August 2026 comparison names that local-first speed and a single opinionated workflow per team as the reason to choose it. ([G2](https://ai.g2.com/product/linear)) ([r/ProductManagement](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProductManagement/comments/1neyq6j/been_using_linear_for_6_months_vs_jira_heres_my/)) ([Breeze](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/compare/linear-vs-jira)) ([Siit](https://www.siit.io/tools/comparison/linear-vs-jira))

Code sits inside that workflow. A GitHub pull request whose branch, title, or body says something like “Fixes ENG-123” moves the Linear issue as the pull request moves, and the 22 October 2026 changelog extends the same pattern to Cursor Origin pull requests. By October 2026, in-app diffs include stacked pull requests, guided reviews are on Basic, coding sessions run in a cloud sandbox and return a reviewable diff, and Loops — scheduled or event-triggered agent workflows on Business and Enterprise — spend AI credits. Coding sessions are priced as the model provider’s token rates plus **$0.25 per 20-minute sandbox block**. The agent layer sits on the issue, the project, and the pull request. ([GitHub](https://linear.app/docs/github)) ([Changelog](https://linear.app/changelog)) ([Linear AI](https://linear.app/ai))

That object is a different thing from a BrownBoard task. Linear’s board is one view of issues grouped by workflow status, alongside lists and timelines. Long-form context is a collaborative Markdown document with live cursors, attached to an issue, project, initiative, team, or cycle. The homepage, the concept model, the documents docs, and the October 2026 changelog describe no pen, shapes, sticky notes, or freeform canvas. Collaboration assumes a company workspace: unlimited members on the free plan, which stops at **2 teams and 250 issues**, after which new issues cannot be created; guests and private teams on Business; SAML, SCIM, and admin controls on Enterprise. Asks, on Business, let someone without a Linear account file a request from Slack, email, or a form, so the outsider enters the tracker. A BrownBoard guest enters the room. The room opens by link; a signed-in account can name, save, claim, and own it. A task there has a title, description, status, urgency, category, images, and a position, and the same task can sit on the drawing canvas. ([Documents](https://linear.app/docs/documents)) ([Pricing](https://linear.app/pricing)) ([Billing and plans](https://linear.app/docs/billing-and-plans)) ([Asks](https://linear.app/asks))

## Reporting and non-software work set Linear’s ceiling

The complaints that recur are the price of that restraint. An August 2026 comparison names reporting depth and coarse permissions as the key limitation. A Jira import does not bring custom workflow states, custom fields, or automation rules, because Linear has no equivalents by design. The September 2025 thread’s top comment treats “use labels” as the practical workaround. A June 2026 Capterra review from an associate director of product says there is no fine-grained control, such as restricting who can mark a task done, and that cross-team, multi-quarter dependencies are where the roadmap starts to struggle. That reviewer also said Linear handles a single product team or five to ten teams well. That is one person’s scope, and it matches how comparisons position the buyer: engineering and product teams, especially at small and mid-size companies, with the case for Jira growing as HR, legal, and IT file into the same system. Linear’s 24 September 2026 answer, for a marketing team that wants different project statuses, is per-team project statuses on Business and Enterprise, still inside one workflow model. G2’s May 2026 cons summary says mobile and analytics could be improved and that comprehensive reporting is missing. Capterra’s public sample is five reviews, too small to treat as a market score. ([Siit](https://www.siit.io/tools/comparison/linear-vs-jira)) ([Breeze](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/compare/linear-vs-jira)) ([r/ProductManagement](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProductManagement/comments/1neyq6j/been_using_linear_for_6_months_vs_jira_heres_my/)) ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10026109/Linear/)) ([Changelog, 24 September 2026](https://linear.app/changelog)) ([G2](https://ai.g2.com/product/linear))

Money moved as the product got larger. PricingSaaS, comparing captures of Linear’s own page, shows yearly Basic rising from **$8 to $10** and Business from **$14 to $16** between 3 October 2025 and 1 January 2026. Legacy Plus and Standard plans move at the next renewal after 1 February 2026, and Linear says most of those customers see a price change. Coding sessions and Loops add a credit meter on top of seats. The free plan is easy to outgrow at 250 issues. Two secondary checks of the pricing page, in late September and early October 2026, report month-to-month rates of $12 and $18. The official page extract used here showed the yearly headlines and did not print a separate monthly figure, so those monthly numbers stay secondary. A G2 listing that still says more than 33,000 organizations is stale against Linear’s own “more than 40,000 companies.” ([PricingSaaS Pulse](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/linear/diffs/2025Q4)) ([Plan changes](https://linear.app/docs/changes-to-linears-pricing-plans)) ([Pricing](https://linear.app/pricing)) ([Saaspare](https://saaspare.com/tools/linear/pricing)) ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10026109/Linear/)) ([G2 listing](https://www.g2.com/it/products/linear/reviews))

Leaving is possible and incomplete. Admins can export issues, projects, and initiatives to CSV, members can export a view up to a cap, and the API and webhooks exist. CSV omits attachment files, guests cannot export issues, and there is no team CSV. Hosting is cloud. The last primary statement found on self-hosting is a Linear employee on Hacker News in October 2022 saying they had no plans to offer it. No later Linear page in this research announced a reversal. Mobile complaints from mid-2025 are a weaker weakness than they first look: the 2026 changelog shows coding sessions, document editing, and iOS fixes shipping through October, so an “abandoned app” claim does not match the current product, even while G2 still lists mobile as a con. ([Exporting data](https://linear.app/docs/exporting-data)) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33199304)) ([Changelog](https://linear.app/changelog))

## Dart turns a sentence into work an agent can take

Dart is Keeple, Inc., a Y Combinator Winter 2022 company still listed as active in San Francisco with a **team of ten**, founders Milad Malek and Zack Swafford. The product sites dartai.com and itsdart.com serve the same homepage, “The workplace for the agent era.” This is a project-management product. A different offering at dart.ceo, a flat-priced company-brain brief, is a separate company, and the Dart programming language is a separate thing entirely. ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dart)) ([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/dart-08b6)) ([itsdart.com](https://itsdart.com/)) ([dart.ceo](https://dart.ceo/))

The durable strength is task grooming. Chat, or a short title, produces tasks, subtasks, filled properties, duplicate detection, and reports. At the February 2024 launch the founders described changelog drafts from completed work and splitting a product spec into subtasks, and they told users to review recommendations before confirming them. Named customers on Dart’s own pages describe that loop in concrete terms: a 90-day MVP plan from ChatGPT landing as tasks “with scary accuracy,” a note that becomes subtasks and deadlines, a longtime Asana user who could work without reading docs, and a marketing assistant on Capterra in June 2026 who said creating and updating tasks “takes almost no effort.” The 2026 product puts agents on top of that structure. A lead agent breaks a goal into a plan. Tasks can be assigned to Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Devin, and other named agents. Chat mode plans, and Build mode executes from a linked task. Layouts beside the board are list, calendar, and roadmap, plus docs, sprints, dependencies, time tracking, and multiple task types. The free Personal plan includes agents, property filling, subtask generation, ChatGPT, Claude, and MCP, for **up to four teammates**. Premium, **$8 per teammate per month** on the yearly price shown, adds AI chat, roadmap planning, Slack, Discord, GitHub, custom statuses, forms, and admin and guest roles. Business at **$12** adds SAML, SCIM, charts, and granular access. Every tier claims unlimited tasks, docs, storage, and AI model usage. The Rundown, with prices checked on 29 August 2026, warns of separate bills from connected agents and model providers, and notes that fair-use limits behind “unlimited” AI were not spelled out. ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39442368)) ([Homepage](https://www.dartai.com/)) ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10018804/Dart/)) ([itsdart.com](https://itsdart.com/)) ([Pricing](https://www.dartai.com/pricing)) ([The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/dart-ai))

Capture is explicit. Forwarding mail to a per-board address creates a task from the subject, body, and attachments. Slack creates a task from `/dart` or the message menu and posts notifications back. GitHub, on Premium, links branches and pull requests through Dart task IDs and can update status when a pull request merges or deploys. The Linear and Jira connections Dart documents are CSV imports. Use-case pages address marketing, sales, customer success, design, HR, and operations along with product and engineering. In February 2024 the founders said users included founders, developers, writers, consultants, and researchers. ([Email](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/email)) ([Slack](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/slack)) ([GitHub](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/github)) ([Linear import](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/linear)) ([itsdart.com](https://itsdart.com/))

BrownBoard and Dart share a board of cards in columns, a dark theme, and a desktop app. They part company at the next action. Dart’s board page describes drag-and-drop, AI-filled properties, and AI brainstorm cards. It describes no pen, shapes, sticky notes, or freeform canvas, and the product pages reviewed never use the word whiteboard. Design help is an agent that generates prototypes from tasks. Dart calls the kanban a board layout and the containers dartboards. The join model is a workspace of teammates, with guests on Premium. BrownBoard’s join model is a link, and the second view of the room is the drawing surface the task cards sit on. A team that wants an agent to create the work, a sprint, a roadmap, or a GitHub-linked task ID is in Dart’s product. A team that wants to sketch and drag the same cards around a canvas is in BrownBoard’s. ([Board layout](https://www.dartai.com/features/board-layout)) ([Features](https://www.dartai.com/features)) ([Pricing](https://www.dartai.com/pricing))

## Unedited AI and a thin tracker cap Dart

Dart has always said a person must edit the machine. In the launch thread the founders said the product would not fully strategize the work, and that users reported saving **about 12% of time spent in project-management tools**. Later company pages say something larger and disagree with each other: the YC profile says **seven hours a week**, a founder bio says about a third of PM time, and Dart’s comparison blog says **seven-plus hours per member**. All of those figures come from Dart. No independent time study appears in the sources. ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39442368)) ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dart)) ([Dart blog](https://www.dartai.com/blog/jira-vs-wrike-vs-dart-which-collaboration-tool-should-you-use))

An AppSumo purchaser on 11 November 2025 put the accuracy problem in one review. AI task creation “initially seems cool,” then produced tasks with “no relation to what I asked or the project I described,” and the reviewer refunded over discomfort about client data going to the AI. AppSumo’s broader set is **4.6 from 56 reviews**, a lifetime-deal sample. A long February 2026 review from a verified purchaser calls the interface immature, the importer limited — dates and comments dropped, everything landing in a general space — the docs lacking folders, and a task unable to live in more than one space. Capterra reviews from May and June 2026 mention sync delays and clunky editing, on a page whose header shows both 4.5 from 6 and 4.7 from 7. Techreviewer’s September 2026 roundup lists G2 at 4.3 from 7. The iOS app, from Keeple, sits at **4.2 from 9 ratings**. These are small samples. The pattern is consistent enough to treat polish, sync, and importer quality as real, mild complaints, and too thin to treat any one score as the market. ([AppSumo review](https://appsumo.com/products/dart/reviews/my-experience-using-dart-366034/)) ([AppSumo reviews](https://appsumo.com/products/dart/reviews/)) ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10018804/Dart/)) ([Techreviewer](https://techreviewer.co/products/dart)) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dart-project-management/id1614157527))

Depth is the other cap, and Dart’s own materials draw it. Jira is described there as the software-development tool, with deeper reporting and a large marketplace. Linear is described as prescriptive, fast, and GitHub-linked for software teams. Dart’s links to both are importers. The engineering integration with live status updates is GitHub, and it is a Premium feature. Slack, in the technical cofounder’s 2024 words, “doesn’t really do anything automated,” which still matches a page that requires a slash command or a message action. Charts, SAML, and SCIM sit on Business, sold by a company whose public listing is ten people. Homepage copy says “Join over **100,000 teams**.” YC still says “**thousands of teams**.” Neither figure was independently counted. The security page states SOC 2 Type II, encryption in transit and at rest, and a claim that AI providers do not train on customer data. The certificate and the subprocessor list were not checked here, and the AppSumo refund shows that a published security page does not, by itself, settle a buyer’s trust. ([Dart blog](https://www.dartai.com/blog/jira-vs-wrike-vs-dart-which-collaboration-tool-should-you-use)) ([GitHub](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/github)) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39442368)) ([Slack](https://www.dartai.com/integrations/slack)) ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dart)) ([itsdart.com](https://itsdart.com/)) ([Security](https://www.dartai.com/security))

## Atlassian splits the board and the canvas across apps

Atlassian’s scale is the strength a startup workspace cannot match, and a filing records it. For the period ended 30 June 2026 the company reported **more than 350,000 customers**, service to **over 85% of the Fortune 500**, operations in **over 200 countries**, and an open platform with **more than 6,000 third-party apps**. A June 2026 Atlassian post said lifetime Marketplace customer spend had passed **$7 billion**, and that **more than 40% of customers** had at least one Marketplace app. Jira’s marketing homepage, fetched the same week, still said “300,000+ companies,” a lower count than the filing. Paid Cloud plans add the operational machinery: Jira Standard goes to **100,000 users** per site, Enterprise to **150 sites**, Premium and Enterprise to **99.9% and 99.95%** uptime SLAs, with data residency and Guard on the upper tiers. Rovo Search, Chat, and Agents are included in paid Jira and Confluence, and whiteboards can call Rovo to diagram and brainstorm using Jira context. Team ’26 claims — Rovo in 75% of the Fortune 500, more than 90% of Atlassian’s enterprise customers, more than 14 million assisted actions in a month — are company blog claims, outside the SEC excerpt. ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000036/team-20260630.htm)) ([Marketplace](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/teamwork/atlassian-marketplace-7-billion)) ([Jira](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira)) ([Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing)) ([Rovo in whiteboards](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/atlassian-intelligence-in-confluence-whiteboards/)) ([Team ’26](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/founder-update-team-26))

The small-team surface inside that suite is Trello, and its strength is the first hour. Free includes unlimited cards, assignees, due dates, and Power-Ups, for **up to 10 collaborators and 10 boards** per Workspace, with 10 MB files and 250 command runs a month. Standard is **$5 per user per month** billed annually, or **$6** monthly. Premium, **$10** annually or **$12.50** monthly, is where Calendar, Timeline, Dashboard, and Map views appear. A guest who sits on a second board in a Standard or Premium Workspace is billed as a member. The Trello plan pages reviewed describe no drawing canvas. ([Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing))

The drawing surface is a Confluence feature. Whiteboards, generally available in April 2024 and Cloud-only, include stickies, lines, sections, stamps, timers, real-time editing, and smart connectors. They can turn a sticky or a shape into a Jira work item, or embed a Jira item, a Confluence page, or a link such as Figma. Atlassian’s stated aim is to consolidate standalone whiteboard tools out of the stack. The caps are per user: **3 active whiteboards** on Free, **10** on Standard, unlimited on Premium and Enterprise. Free Confluence is 10 users, 2 GB, and no space or page permissions. Paid Confluence offers up to **five free guests per paid user**, each guest limited to a single space. A May 2025 community post described a **10,000-element** limit and Miro and Mural importers. That element cap was not reconfirmed for October 2026. Turning a sticky into a Jira work item is a conversion: the drawn mark and the tracked issue are linked records, and the kanban lives in another app. ([Confluence whiteboards](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/whiteboards)) ([What’s new](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/features/whats-new)) ([Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing)) ([May 2025 whiteboard updates](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Confluence-Whiteboards-articles/May-2025-Whiteboard-Updates/ba-p/3040812))

Jira is a third category. It is the system of record and, in 2026 marketing, the place work is assigned to coding agents, with backlog, board, timeline, calendar, roles on Standard, and cross-team planning and approvals on Premium. Free Jira is **10 users**, 2 GB, and **150 automation steps** per subscription per month. The quota then rises to 400, 750, and 1,000 steps per user per month on Standard, Premium, and Enterprise. Rovo Search, Chat, and Agents are listed on Standard. Jira Product Discovery is another Cloud app, for ideas and roadmaps linked to Jira issues, free for up to **3 creators**, with a product-page claim of **25,000+ customers**. Teamwork Collection bills Jira, Confluence, and Loom together. Trello and Jira Product Discovery sit outside that bundle. A simple board plus a whiteboard means at least two products. The pages reviewed show no single Atlassian object that is both a room’s kanban and that room’s canvas. ([Jira](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira)) ([Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing)) ([Jira Product Discovery](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/product-discovery)) ([Collections billing](https://support.atlassian.com/subscriptions-and-billing/docs/how-billing-works-for-collections/))

The bill is hard to read from a seat price. Atlassian’s Cloud FAQ gives the post-13 October 2026 monthly ladder for Jira Standard as **$9.70** per user for the first 100, **$8.20** for users 101–250, and **$6.85** for 251–450, with a worked example of 450 users at **$3,570 a month**. An annual example prices the 500-user tier at **$39,000**. Secondary summaries describe the first-band move as about 7%, from $9.05. The FAQ page was still worded in the future tense when fetched on 27 October 2026, after the date it names. Confluence’s own pricing page displayed Standard at $5.78 and Premium at $11.16 per user per month, which conflicts with secondary monthly list prices of $7.15 and $14.10. The billing toggle was not preserved, so Confluence’s dollar price is unsettled here. The feature gates on that page did render. Rovo credits are **25, 70, and 150 per user per month** on Jira or Confluence Standard, Premium, and Enterprise, ten times that on the collections, with overage list-priced at **$0.01 per credit**. Extra-usage billing is dated **3 December 2026**. Support says extra usage is on by default. The price FAQ says it requires opt-in. Both pages were live. BrownBoard’s two views are one room. Atlassian’s two closest views are Trello and a Confluence whiteboard, each with a printed free cap, and each still starting from an account, a Workspace or a site, and a plan. Roles and permissions start on Jira Standard, cross-team planning starts on Premium, and anonymous access to spaces is a site feature on that same Standard extract. The buyer on Atlassian’s pages is an organization with SLAs, residency, a Marketplace, and a Fortune 500 install base. The buyer in BrownBoard’s product is a person who sends a link.

## Conclusion

Through 2026 the shared move was to attach an agent to a structured task. Linear prices a sandbox that returns a diff. Dart assigns the task to a coding agent and calls the product a workplace. Atlassian meters Rovo against data the customer already stored, with overage billing dated 3 December 2026. That move deepens the system of record. It leaves the working session — the hour when people draw, stick notes, and drag the same cards around one room — outside the product each company is now optimizing.

The sources reviewed never place BrownBoard in a win or a loss against any of the three, which is what an early product should expect. The decision that follows from the products themselves is which direction a room grows. A room that becomes small-team task tracking meets Linear’s free cap of 250 issues, Dart’s free cap of four teammates with grooming and docs already included, and Trello’s free cap of ten boards. A room that stays a link, a board, and a canvas occupies the gap left after Atlassian spent 2024 through 2026 pulling Miro and Mural into Confluence. That effort still ships as a wiki feature, a per-user cap, and a convert-to-Jira step. Software teams can keep both: the room for the session, Linear or Jira for what has to ship. Those uses sit side by side. They collide if the room takes on issue identity, sprints, and agent execution, the job those products already own.
