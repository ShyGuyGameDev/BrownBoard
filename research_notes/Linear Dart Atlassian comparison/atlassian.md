# Atlassian work suite vs BrownBoard (October 2026)

Research date: 27 October 2026. BrownBoard facts below are the product description supplied in this research brief, not an independent public source. Prices and plan gates move; where Atlassian’s own pages disagreed with each other or with secondary write-ups, both are recorded.

## What is the 2025–2026 Atlassian work suite, and what did Atlassian recently push?

### Takeaway
Atlassian sells work tracking and collaboration as several Cloud apps plus bundled “collections,” not as one board. Jira is the system of record (and, in 2026 marketing, the control plane for AI agents). Confluence is the wiki, and whiteboards are a Confluence feature with edition caps. Trello remains a separate, simpler board product and is not part of the Teamwork Collection. Jira Product Discovery is a separate Cloud app for ideas and roadmaps that links into Jira. Rovo is the shared AI layer, included in paid Cloud plans and metered with credits; extra-usage billing is scheduled for 3 December 2026. The 2024–2026 push is AI-in-the-workflow, whiteboard parity with standalone tools, and packaging Jira + Confluence + Loom as one subscription.

### Inferences
- By October 2026 the work suite a small team would actually compare is four surfaces plus a meter: Jira (issues, plans, agents), Trello (simple boards), Confluence (docs and whiteboards), and Jira Product Discovery (ideas and roadmaps), with Rovo credits sitting on the paid Cloud plans. Bitbucket and Loom change the picture only when the buyer wants the repo or the video bundle, not when they want a shared board.
- “Consolidation” did not collapse these into one product. It bundled Jira, Confluence, and Loom into Teamwork Collection and left Trello, Jira Product Discovery, and Bitbucket as separate purchases. Whiteboards were pulled into Confluence (generally available April 2024, then AI, importers, and higher edition limits through 2025–2026) specifically to replace tools like Miro and Mural.
- The commercial story flipped twice in 2025–2026: April 2025 messaging said Rovo should be included rather than an extra SKU; by late 2026 included use is capped, and overage billing is dated 3 December 2026. List prices also stepped up on 13 October 2026.

## What are the suite’s greatest strengths?

### Takeaway
The strengths that matter against a small collaborative workspace are scale and procurement trust, one vendor for tracking plus docs plus a whiteboard, deep configuration once a team needs process, a large Marketplace, and an AI layer aimed at the data already in those tools. Trello’s strength is the opposite: a board a non-admin can use immediately, with a real free tier.

### Cited Findings
- Scale and trust, from Atlassian’s own SEC filing (period ended 30 June 2026): more than 350,000 customers, over 85% of the Fortune 500, more than 6,000 Marketplace apps and integrations, Cloud as the primary commercial offering, plus Government Cloud and Isolated Cloud mentioned in that filing’s risk discussion. — [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000036/team-20260630.htm)
- Jira plan gates that only show up when a company is large: up to 100,000 users per site on Standard and above, up to 150 sites on Enterprise, 99.9% and 99.95% uptime SLAs on Premium and Enterprise, data residency on paid Jira and Confluence, and Guard Standard included on Enterprise (SSO, SCIM, and related controls). Confluence’s table matches the SLA, site, and Guard pattern, with up to 250,000 users per site on paid Confluence plans. — [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing); [Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing)
- Docs, tracking, and a whiteboard from one vendor, without a second whiteboard contract: whiteboards ship inside Confluence and can create Jira work items; Teamwork Collection bills Jira, Confluence, and Loom as one subscription; the Jira homepage tells buyers to add Confluence and Loom. — [Confluence whiteboards](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/whiteboards); [collections billing](https://support.atlassian.com/subscriptions-and-billing/docs/how-billing-works-for-collections/); [Jira](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira)

Confluence Standard adds space and page permissions; Free does not. - AI distribution is broad relative to a startup workspace: Rovo Search, Chat, and Agents are included in paid Jira and Confluence rather than sold as a separate seat in the April 2025 announcement, and Team ’26 claims heavy enterprise use (75% of the Fortune 500, more than 90% of Atlassian enterprise customers, more than 14 million assisted actions in the prior month). ### Inferences
- The six strengths to keep in a comparison are: (1) enterprise proof and compliance packaging, (2) Jira’s workflow and permission depth, (3) Confluence plus Jira plus whiteboards from one vendor, (4) Marketplace coverage for the gaps Jira does not ship, (5) Trello as the simple on-ramp, and (6) Rovo sitting on data the customer already stored in those products. Strength (6) is real only for paid Cloud customers on a company domain, and it is metered.
- Against BrownBoard, strengths (1), (2), (4), and (6) are a different buying job. Strengths (3) and (5) are the actual product overlap, and they are split across two apps.

### Gaps
- No independent audit of the “14 million Rovo-assisted actions” or “75% of the Fortune 500 use Rovo” claims was found outside Atlassian’s blog. The SEC filing supports the broader “85% of the Fortune 500” customer claim, not the Rovo-specific percentages.
- “Less expensive than most whiteboard tools” is Atlassian’s wording. No dollar comparison was published on the whiteboard page.
- G2 satisfaction scores for Jira, Trello, and Confluence were not retrieved.

## What are the greatest weaknesses, and how do Jira, Trello, and Confluence differ?

### Takeaway
Jira’s repeated problem is the cost of configuration: permissions, workflows, admin time, edition gates, and a price that stepped up in October 2026, with AI overages scheduled for December 2026. Trello’s problem is a ceiling: the free Workspace stops at 10 boards and 10 collaborators, guests on a second board become billable, and timeline or dashboard views sit on Premium. Confluence’s problem is that the whiteboard is a capped feature inside a wiki, not a shared room, and the wiki itself has a permissions and edition model a small team does not need. Product sprawl is the suite-level weakness: the simple board, the whiteboard, the issue tracker, and the roadmap are different products with different bills.

### Cited Findings
- Jira complexity and admin cost, as far as this research could source it without opening G2 or Reddit: Breeze writes that Jira’s learning curve is work-item types, workflows, schemes, fields, and permissions, that a team-managed project is simpler than a company-managed one, and that “most complaints about Jira are complaints about a particular instance nobody has pruned in years.” It also says Trello gets harder at scale because workspace-level Table and Calendar views are Premium. — [Breeze](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/compare/jira-vs-trello)
- Planyway, a planning add-on vendor, contrasts the products this way: Trello setup in minutes versus Jira setup that chooses project type, workflow, issue types, and fields; Trello cards move freely between lists, while Jira workflows can block a transition until conditions are met; Trello has no built-in dependencies and a flat card structure; Jira has epic-to-subtask hierarchy, JQL, and Scrum objects (backlog, sprints, story points). It places Calendar, Timeline, Table, Dashboard, and Map on Trello Premium, matching Trello’s pricing page. Treat the Jira workflow and sprint details as this vendor’s description, consistent with how Jira is sold, not as a quote from an Atlassian feature page fetched here. — [Planyway](https://planyway.com/blog/trello-vs-jira-comparison-guide); [Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing)
- ClickUp’s 2026 “Jira alternatives” article claims five complaint themes from G2, Reddit’s r/projectmanagement, and Atlassian Community: a full-time admin for permissions and boards, non-engineering teams refusing Jira, add-on cost (it cites Premium “Advanced Roadmaps” and a Tempo time-tracking example), a weak mobile app, and automation quotas. The underlying reviews were not opened. ### Inferences
- Jira’s weakness is weight. The product assumes an admin, a scheme, and a paid edition before cross-team planning, high automation, or enterprise identity are available. Price increases and the December 2026 credit meter add a bill that is hard to predict from the seat price alone.
- Trello’s weakness is the ceiling, not the first hour. The free Workspace and the lack of enforced workflow are fine for a small queue. Reporting views, multi-board guests, and anything that looks like structure are where Atlassian asks the team to pay more or to leave for Jira.
- Confluence’s weakness, for a board-plus-canvas product, is that the whiteboard is attached to a documentation system. Edition caps, space permissions, and “make a Jira issue from this sticky” are the collaboration model. Slow-page complaints were not sourced in this pass and are not asserted here.
- Sprawl is the weakness that shows up when someone tries to get BrownBoard’s two views by buying Atlassian: they buy at least Trello and Confluence, and they still do not get one room.

### Gaps
- Verified 2024–2026 G2 or Reddit complaint quotes were not collected. ClickUp’s quotations are unverified and come from a competitor.
- No sourced 2025–2026 measurement of Jira or Confluence UI slowness was found. “Slow UX” should not be stated as a fact from this note.
- Jira’s guest policy (whether Free has zero guests, and whether paid plans include five guests per user) rests on a third-party calculator, not on the Atlassian pricing extract. Confluence’s five-guest rule is from Atlassian.
- Whether Trello Enterprise multi-board guests are free or still billable was not fully specified. The billing FAQ quoted here names Standard and Premium. Enterprise lists “multi-board guests” as a feature without a price line.

## How is Atlassian different from BrownBoard?

### Takeaway
BrownBoard is one shared room with two views of the same work, opened by a link. Atlassian is a catalog: a board product, a wiki with a whiteboard, an issue tracker, a discovery app, and an AI meter, sold to an organization with admins, editions, and seat counts. The small-team path into Atlassian still starts with an account, a Workspace or a site, and a plan limit, not with a link a guest can open and draw on.

### Cited Findings
- BrownBoard, as specified for this comparison (October 2026): shared rooms opened by link; guests can join; signed-in accounts can name, save, claim, and own rooms. Each room has a kanban and a freeform whiteboard. Kanban tasks have a title, description, status, urgency, category, images, and position; categories are the columns. The whiteboard is a drawing canvas (pen, shapes, text, sticky notes, images, connectors, and task cards on the canvas linked back to the kanban). Realtime sync via Supabase. Web app plus an Electron desktop shell. Dark mode. It does not have sprints, a workflow and permissions matrix, a wiki, a marketplace, or enterprise admin.
- Atlassian’s closest free entry points are capped products, not an open room. Trello Free: 10 collaborators, 10 boards, 10 MB files, 250 automation runs. Confluence Free: 10 users, 3 active whiteboards per user, no space or page permissions, 2 GB. Jira Free: 10 users, 2 GB, 150 automation steps per site per month, and Rovo Search, Chat, and Agents listed as Standard, not Free. — [Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing); [Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing); [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing)
- Outsider access is an administered exception. Trello bills a guest who sits on more than one board. Confluence’s free guests exist on paid plans, up to five per paid user, one space each. Jira Standard’s pricing extract describes anonymous access to spaces, which is a site feature, not a link to a single room. Rovo itself requires a verified company domain. — [Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing); [Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing); [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing); [Rovo credits](https://support.atlassian.com/rovo/docs/rovo-usage-limits/)
- The two views are not one object in Atlassian. Trello is the board and has no drawing surface in the plan descriptions reviewed. Confluence whiteboards are the drawing surface and live in a page tree; Jira work items are created from stickies or linked with smart connectors. Getting both means at least two products, and the task a person draws is not automatically the same record as the card unless someone converts or links it. — [Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing); [Confluence whiteboards](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/whiteboards)
- Configuration is the default Atlassian posture and the non-goal for BrownBoard. Jira Standard is where roles and permissions start. Jira Premium is where cross-team planning and approvals start. Confluence Free cannot set space or page permissions; Standard can. None of that exists in the BrownBoard brief. — [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing); [Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing)
- The buyer Atlassian’s pages address is an organization: SLAs, data residency, Guard, 100,000 users, multiple sites, a Marketplace, and a Fortune 500 install base. BrownBoard’s brief addresses a person who sends a link. — [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000036/team-20260630.htm); [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing)

### Inferences
- Many products versus one room is the primary difference, not a missing sticker set. A BrownBoard room is the board and the canvas. An Atlassian “equivalent” is a Trello or Jira board plus a Confluence whiteboard, plus the decision of who is a billable user.
- Immediate drawing versus configuration: BrownBoard’s first action is a pen, a sticky, or a card. Jira’s first serious action is a project type, a workflow, and a permission scheme. Trello is closer to immediate, until the team hits 10 boards or wants a timeline.
- Enterprise buyer versus a link: Atlassian’s trust story (Fortune 500 coverage, SLAs, residency, Guard, Marketplace) is the reason a company stays. It is also the reason a friend-to-friend session is a poor fit. Guests exist, and they are still an admin concept with caps.

### Gaps
- No public BrownBoard pricing or user counts were in scope. The comparison uses the product brief only.
- Whether a Confluence whiteboard and a Jira board can be kept in live two-way sync for the same items, beyond “create a work item from a sticky” and smart connectors, was not documented on the pages fetched.

## Which Atlassian surface overlaps BrownBoard, and which is a different category?

### Takeaway
The real overlap is Trello (a simple kanban of cards and columns) and Confluence whiteboards (a freeform canvas with stickies, shapes, and connectors). They overlap one at a time, in different products, with different caps. Jira is a different category: a configurable work-item tracker and, in 2026, an agent control plane, with sprints, workflows, permissions, and scale limits BrownBoard does not try to match. Jira Product Discovery is also a different category: prioritization and roadmaps for people who already track delivery in Jira.

### Cited Findings
- Trello overlap: lists and cards, assignees, due dates, checklists, custom fields on Standard, Power-Ups, and a board you can open without designing a workflow. Limits that BrownBoard’s brief does not have: 10 boards and 10 collaborators on Free, billable multi-board guests, views held back until Premium, no drawing tools on the pricing page. — [Trello pricing](https://trello.com/pricing)
- Confluence whiteboard overlap: freeform stickies, lines, sections, shapes, text-like labels, images, timers, real-time editing, dark mode (shipped May 2024), public links (July 2024), and connectors. Jira cards appear when someone converts a sticky or uses smart connectors, not as the same room’s kanban. Active-whiteboard caps are 3 per user on Free and 10 on Standard. Cloud only. — [Confluence whiteboards](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/whiteboards); [what’s new](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/features/whats-new); [Confluence pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/pricing)
- Jira is sold as the system of record for plans, delivery, and agents: backlog, board, timeline, calendar, roles and permissions, cross-team planning on Premium, anonymous external access on Standard, up to 100,000 users, and MCP/CLI context for coding agents. A testimonial on the Jira homepage treats sprints as normal Jira use, including for marketing. Secondary comparisons add workflow conditions, JQL, and epic hierarchy as the capabilities Trello does not have. That is a software and operations tracker, not a link-opened drawing room. — [Jira](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira); [Jira pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing); [Planyway](https://planyway.com/blog/trello-vs-jira-comparison-guide)
- Jira Product Discovery overlaps neither the canvas nor the casual board. It is ideas, scores, matrices, and roadmaps, with a billed creator role, tied to Jira issues and Confluence pages. Atlassian’s own positioning is “from ideas to impact” and “connect to delivery in Jira.” — [Jira Product Discovery](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/product-discovery)
- Rovo, Bitbucket, and Loom do not create a third board-level overlap. Rovo is an AI layer on the suite, including a panel on Confluence whiteboards. Loom adds async video that can spawn Jira items and Confluence pages. Bitbucket is source control, relevant if the competitor is a full software stack, not if the competitor is a room with a board and a canvas. — [Rovo in whiteboards](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/atlassian-intelligence-in-confluence-whiteboards/); [Teamwork Collection introduction](https://www.atlassian.com/collections/teamwork/guides/introduction); [Cloud pricing FAQ](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/future-pricing/cloud/list/faqs)

### Inferences
- If the comparison is “where would a small collaborative team feel BrownBoard,” the honest Atlassian answers are Trello first and a Confluence whiteboard second. They are cousins, not substitutes for each other, and neither one is both views at once.
- If the comparison is “where would a software org feel BrownBoard,” the answer is that they would not. They would feel Jira, and they would accept admin cost, seat price, and Marketplace apps in exchange for workflow control, auditability, and agent governance. That trade is outside BrownBoard’s stated product.
- Jira Product Discovery is a third category again: upstream of Jira, not a simpler Jira and not a whiteboard.

### Gaps
- No page fetched shows a single Atlassian object that is both the kanban and the whiteboard the way BrownBoard describes one room. Absence from these pages is not a formal proof that no beta exists; it is what the current product and pricing pages emphasize.
- A current, official Jira feature page listing sprints, issue types, and JQL in Atlassian’s words was not fetched. Sprint and JQL claims here rely on the Jira homepage testimonial and on secondary comparisons.
